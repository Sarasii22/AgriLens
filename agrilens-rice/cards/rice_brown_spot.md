## Summary
Brown spot is a fungal disease caused by Cochliobolus miyabeanus (also called Bipolaris oryzae). It can attack many parts of the rice plant: the young shoot at seedling stage, leaves, leaf sheaths, the branches of the grain head, the husks and the grains.

## How to recognise brown spot
- The spots can be tiny dark brown or reddish-brown dots, or large oval to round spots.
- Large spots have a dark brown edge and a lighter reddish-brown or grey centre.
- Spots on the leaf sheath and on the husks look similar to those on the leaves.
- If the husks are infected, the grains can show a general black discolouration.

## What a farmer can do
- Leaf spots alone often do not cause serious yield loss. The bigger loss happens when the fungus reaches the grain head and the grains, so watch the crop as it heads.
- Keep the crop well fed. Plants that are short of nutrients tend to get more brown spot. Follow the Department of Agriculture fertiliser recommendation.
- Use clean, healthy seed.
- Avoid letting the crop become stressed, for example by long dry periods without water.

## When to get help
- If many leaves are covered or the grains are turning dark, contact your nearest Department of Agriculture or agrarian services officer.
- Ask whether a fungicide is worth using and which one is approved for your area.
