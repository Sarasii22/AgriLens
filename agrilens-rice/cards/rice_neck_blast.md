## Summary
Neck blast is the form of rice blast (the same fungus that causes leaf blast) that attacks the neck, which is the part of the stem just below the grain head. It is usually the most damaging form because it can stop the grains from filling.

## How to recognise neck blast
- The neck below the grain head turns brown to black.
- The grain head may bend or break at the neck.
- The grain head can look pale or bleached, with poorly filled or empty grains.
- It often appears around flowering and grain filling, sometimes after leaf blast was seen earlier in the season.

## What a farmer can do
- Use rice varieties that the Department of Agriculture recommends for your area, preferably with blast resistance.
- Do not apply too much nitrogen fertiliser. Follow the recommended rate.
- Keep the field and the bunds free of weeds.
- Check the field regularly as the crop gets close to flowering.
- Ask for advice early. Protection is usually planned before or around flowering, and it is much harder to help a grain head that has already died.

## When to get help
- If you see brown or black necks on several plants, contact your nearest Department of Agriculture or agrarian services officer quickly.
- Tell the officer which growth stage your crop is at, because the right advice depends on it.
