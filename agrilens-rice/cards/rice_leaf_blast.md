## Summary
Rice blast is a fungal disease caused by Magnaporthe oryzae (also called Pyricularia). It is named after the part of the plant it attacks: leaf blast on the leaves, node blast on the stem joints, and neck or panicle blast at the base of the grain head.

## How to recognise leaf blast
- The spots on the leaves are spindle-shaped: wide in the middle and pointed at both ends.
- Each spot has a brown, reddish-brown or yellowish-brown edge and a grey or ash-coloured centre.
- A fully grown spot is about 1 to 1.5 cm long and 0.3 to 0.5 cm wide.

## What a farmer can do
- Plant rice varieties that the Department of Agriculture recommends for your area, preferably varieties with blast resistance.
- Do not apply too much nitrogen fertiliser. Follow the recommended rate.
- Keep the field and the bunds free of weeds.
- Walk through the field often, especially in humid or cloudy weather, so you notice spreading early.

## When to get help
- If many leaves have spots or the spots are spreading fast, contact your nearest Department of Agriculture or agrarian services officer. They can tell you whether a fungicide is needed and which one is approved.
- Leaf blast can be followed by neck blast, which attacks the grain head and does more damage.
