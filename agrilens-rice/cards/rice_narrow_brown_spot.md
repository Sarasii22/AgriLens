## Summary
Narrow brown spot is a fungal disease caused by Sphaerulina oryzina (also called Cercospora janseana). It attacks the leaf blades, the leaf sheaths, the small stalks of the grain head and the husks. How bad it gets changes from year to year and depends on the rice variety, and it usually becomes worse as the plants get close to ripening.

## How to recognise narrow brown spot
- The spots on the leaves are narrow, straight lines that are light to dark brown.
- The lines run in the same direction as the leaf veins.
- Each line is only about 2 to 10 mm long and 1 to 1.5 mm wide.
- On very susceptible varieties the lines can join up and form long brown dead areas.
- On the leaf sheath it can make a net-like pattern of brown and pale yellow-brown patches.

## What a farmer can do
- Check the crop more carefully as it gets close to ripening, because the disease is worse then.
- Follow the Department of Agriculture fertiliser recommendation and avoid very heavy use of nitrogen.
- Keep weeds under control and avoid sowing the seed too thickly.
- Ask your officer about rice varieties that cope better with this disease.

## When to get help
- If the brown lines are spreading across most leaves and sheaths, contact your nearest Department of Agriculture or agrarian services officer.
- Ask whether any treatment is needed. It may not be worth treating a light infection.
