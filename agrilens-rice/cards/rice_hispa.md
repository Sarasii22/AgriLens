## Summary
Rice hispa is damage caused by a small beetle. It is an insect pest, not a fungal disease. Both the adult beetles and their young (larvae) feed on rice leaves.

## How to recognise hispa damage
- Adult beetles scrape off the green surface of the leaf, leaving white or silvery streaks that run along the leaf.
- The young larvae tunnel inside the leaf and make pale, blister-like patches that later dry out.
- Badly damaged leaves can look dry and scorched, especially in young crops.
- You may see small dark beetles with tiny spines on the leaves.

## What a farmer can do
- Walk the field and look closely at the leaves for beetles and fresh streaks.
- Keep the bunds and field edges clear of grass weeds, because the beetles can shelter there.
- Avoid putting on too much nitrogen fertiliser, which makes the leaves more attractive to insects.
- Do not spray without advice, because the wrong spray can harm helpful insects and waste money.

## When to get help
- If many leaves are streaked or drying, contact your nearest Department of Agriculture or agrarian services officer.
- Ask which control is recommended for your area and when to use it.
